\begin{abstract}
We construct a rational eighteen-dimensional polytope whose iterated
products and joins have projection-volume root limit strictly between
$2.9280$ and $2.9283$. Together with a prior upper bound below $2.855$,
this proves a strict exponential separation from the entire class
generated from a point by products, joins, and invertible affine maps.
Finite rational recipes and a fixed-block extension also exceed that
class in every dimension at least nine. The principal seed is a
codimension-four section of the standard simplex, specified by twenty-three
integer four-vectors. Simpler sections in dimensions eight, twelve, and
fifteen give comparison rates. The constructions use the existing
simplex-section method, product--join calculus, and balanced recursion;
the new geometric inputs yield the stronger certified lower rate and
whole-class separation. The proof combines facet determinant identities,
exponential-spline calculations, and exact recurrence bounds. A versioned
data and code repository provides the coordinates, finite witnesses, and
verification programs. The result establishes a limitation of the
point-generated class, while leaving unrestricted optimality and the
minimal separation dimension open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The separation problem and main result}
For a full-dimensional convex body $K\subset\R^n$, use the normalization
\[
 h_{\Pi K}(u)=\vol_{n-1}(K\mid u^\perp),\qquad
 R_n(K)=\frac{\vol_n(\Pi K)}{\vol_n(K)^{n-1}}.
\]
Here $u$ is a unit vector and $K\mid u^\perp$ is its orthogonal projection.
Write $g(n)=n^n/n!$ and $c_n=(n+1)g(n)=R_n(T_n)$, where $T_n$ is an
$n$-simplex. The ratio is invariant under invertible affine maps.
For standard background on support functions, area measures, and affine
convex geometry, see Schneider~\cite{schneider}.
We omit the dimension subscript when it is unambiguous.
The standard product and join are taken in independent coordinate spaces:
\[
 A*B=\conv\bigl((A,0,0)\cup(0,B,1)\bigr).
\]
Let $\CC$ consist of polytopes obtained from a zero-dimensional point by
finitely many Cartesian products, joins, and invertible affine maps on
their affine hulls. Rank-dropping affine maps are outside this definition.
Set $\CC_n=\{K\in\CC:\dim K=n\}$ and
\[
 \Gamma_{\CC}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{K\in\CC_n}R_n(K)^{1/n}.
\]
The limit in fact exists by the spectral reduction in~\cite{mxym-spectral},
but the limsup definition suffices below.
Let $U_n$ be the supremum of $R_n(K)^{1/n}$ over all full-dimensional
convex bodies $K\subset\R^n$.

The unrestricted maximization problem has a longer history.
Brannen~\cite{brannen} formulated the simplex maximum conjecture and
disproved a different proposed upper bound for centrally symmetric bodies.
Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang~\cite{lyz} developed affine-invariant functionals
for polytopes and derived projection-volume estimates. Saroglou~\cite{saroglou-cones}
established sharp three-dimensional maxima within the classes of zonoids,
cones, and double cones. These results concern the same volume ratio,
but do not supply the whole-class comparison used here.

Feng, Hu, Liu, and Xu~\cite{feng} already disprove the simplex upper bound
in every dimension at least nine using polytopes with at most $n+2$
facets. OpenAI's product example~\cite{openai} provides a particularly
simple later counterexample. Subsequent work of Zhang (repository account mxym)
gives the exact product--join calculus and a recursive construction with
root limit between $2.8534$ and $2.8535$~\cite[Section 9]{mxym-calculus}.
Its mixed Bellman bound~\cite{mxym-upper} controls every finite expression
in $\CC$, not just that particular recursion.
The question addressed here is whether the point-generated closure itself
can attain the best unrestricted exponential rate.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is an explicitly specified sequence of rational polytopes
$P_n\subset\R^n$ such that
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}R_n(P_n)^{1/n}=\beta,
 \qquad 2.9280<\beta<2.9283.
\]
Consequently, using the prior upper theorem~\cite{mxym-upper},
\[
 \Gamma_{\CC}<2.855<\beta
 \le\liminf_{n\to\infty}U_n.
\]
There is also an explicit sequence $E_n$, given by finite product--join
recipes and a fixed-block extension, satisfying
\[
 R_n(E_n)>\sup_{K\in\CC_n}R_n(K)\qquad\text{for every }n\ge9.
\]
The sequence $P_n$ can be chosen to satisfy the same strict inequality
for every $n\ge9$. No minimal-dimension claim is made.
\end{theorem}

Thus the advantage concerns an entire construction class. For example,
the asymptotic ratio of $R_n(P_n)$ to its class supremum has lower root
at least $2.928/2.855>1.0255$. This is an exponential separation;
it is not a claim of optimality among all convex bodies.
Table~\ref{tab:seeds} summarizes the seed constructions. The proof first
computes their geometric states, then certifies the amplified limits,
and finally compares them with the imported class upper bound.

For context, G.~Zhang's reverse polar projection inequality~\cite{zhang}
and the Blaschke--Santal\'o inequality~\cite{meyer-pajor} give
\[
 R_n(K)\le\frac{\kappa_n^2 n^n}{\binom{2n}{n}},\qquad
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}U_n\le\frac{\pi e}{2},
\]
where $\kappa_n$ is the volume of the Euclidean unit ball; see
\cite[Introduction, equation (3)]{saroglou} for the combined estimate.
The limiting constant follows
from Stirling's formula. A substantial unrestricted gap therefore remains.

\section{Facet invariants and inherited operation rules}
For a polytope $K$ of dimension $d$ and volume $V$, let $v_i$ be its
outward facet area-normal vectors and let $\zeta_i$ be the facet area times
its support number. Equivalently, $\langle v_i,x\rangle=\zeta_i$ on facet $i$.
Define the determinant sums
\[
 \RK(K)=\sum_{|J|=d}|\det(v_j:j\in J)|,\qquad
 \mathcal S(K)=\sum_{|J|=d+1}|\det((v_j,\zeta_j):j\in J)|.
\]
Cauchy's projection formula gives $\RK(K)=\vol_d(\Pi K)$: the projection
body is a translate of the zonotope $\sum_i[0,v_i]$.
The determinant expression for zonotope volume and its application to
projection bodies are classical; see~\cite[Section 1]{lyz}
and~\cite[Section 3]{saroglou}.
The lifted invariant of~\cite[Section 2]{mxym-calculus} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant}
 a(K)=\frac{\mathcal S(K)}{dV\RK(K)},\qquad
 H(K)=\frac1{a(K)},\qquad Q(K)=\frac{a(K)R_d(K)}{g(d)}.
\end{equation}
It is affine invariant and satisfies $1/(d+1)\le a(K)\le1$.
We use different symbols for the geometric tilt parameters below and
the invariant $a$.

For positive-dimensional factors $A,B$ of dimensions $r,s$, the exact
identities in~\cite[Sections 3--5]{mxym-calculus} are
\begin{align}
 R(A\times B)&=R(A)R(B),&
 a(A\times B)&=\frac{ra(A)+sa(B)}{r+s},\label{eq:product}\\
 R(A*B)&=\frac{g(r+s+1)}{g(r)g(s)}R(A)R(B)(a(A)+a(B)),&
 a(A*B)&=\frac{a(A)a(B)}{a(A)+a(B)}.\label{eq:join}
\end{align}
These rules apply to the nonsimplex seeds as well as to members of
$\CC$. They, and the balanced recursion used below, are imported prior
results rather than new operation formulas.

\section{An explicit rational seed}
Let $n=2m$ with $m\ge1$, choose $0<u_1<\cdots<u_m<1$, and put
\[
 b=(-u_m,\ldots,-u_1,u_1,\ldots,u_m),\qquad
 K(u)=\{x\ge0:\ \boldsymbol1\cdot x+b\cdot x\le1,
                         \ \boldsymbol1\cdot x-b\cdot x\le1\}.
\]
For rational $u$, this is a rational realization of the central two-facet
section family underlying~\cite[Section 4]{feng}. We do not claim the family itself.
The symmetry exchanging paired coordinates interchanges the two upper
facets; it is not central symmetry of $K(u)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:seed}
The polytope $K(u)$ has $n+1+m^2$ vertices and $n+2$ facets. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:I}
 I(u)=\sum_{i=1}^m
 \frac{u_i^{2m-2}}{(1+u_i)\prod_{j\ne i}(u_i^2-u_j^2)},\qquad
 p(u)=n+1+\sum_{j=1}^m(2j-1)u_j,\qquad
 s(u)=1+\sum_{j=1}^m u_j.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seedstate}
 n!\vol_n(K(u))=I(u),\qquad
 R_n(K(u))=g(n)I(u)p(u),\qquad a(K(u))=\frac{s(u)}{p(u)}.
\end{equation}
In particular $Q(K(u))=I(u)s(u)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The vertices are the origin, the $n$ points
$e_i/(1+|b_i|)$, and, for each pair $b_i=-v<0<b_j=w$,
\[
 \frac{w}{v+w}e_i+\frac{v}{v+w}e_j.
\]
An active-set argument proves completeness: at a nonzero vertex one or
both upper facets must be active, leaving at most one or two positive
coordinates, respectively. In the latter case the signs must be opposite.
Each coordinate hyperplane contains the origin and $n-1$ independent axis
vertices. Each upper hyperplane contains a point with all coordinates
positive and $b\cdot x$ of the required strict sign. Thus every listed
inequality defines a facet.

On $b\cdot x\ge0$, the upper constraint with $+b$ determines feasibility;
the other upper constraint is redundant there.
Change variables $y_i=(1+b_i)x_i$ and use the standard simplex with slack
coordinate $y_0=1-\sum_{i=1}^n y_i$. Its truncating coefficients are
$t_0=0$ and $t_i=b_i/(1+b_i)$. The Jacobian is
$\prod_i(1+b_i)^{-1}$ and
$t_i-t_j=(b_i-b_j)/((1+b_i)(1+b_j))$ for $i,j\ge1$.
The simplex truncation identity in Appendix~\ref{app:truncation}, with
these factors cancelled, gives
\[
 n!\vol_n(K(u)\cap\{b\cdot x\ge0\})
 =\sum_{b_i>0}\frac{b_i^{n-1}}{(1+b_i)\prod_{j\ne i}(b_i-b_j)}.
\]
The two halves have equal volume. Pairing $b_j=\pm u_j$ in the last
denominator gives~\eqref{eq:I}.

Write $V=\vol_n(K(u))$. Each upper facet vector is a positive multiple
of $\boldsymbol1\pm b$. Reflection makes the two multiples equal.
The divergence identity $\sum_i\zeta_i=nV$, with zero support on coordinate
facets, makes each multiple $nV/2$. Balance of area-normal vectors then
gives $-nVe_i$ on coordinate facet $i$. After division by $nV$, the
horizontal generators and their lifted support coordinates are
\[
 (-e_i,0),\qquad
 \left(\frac{\boldsymbol1+b}{2},\frac12\right),\quad
 \left(\frac{\boldsymbol1-b}{2},\frac12\right).
\]
The horizontal $n$-minor sum is
\[
 1+n+\frac12\sum_{i<j}|b_i-b_j|
 =n+1+\sum_{j=1}^m(2j-1)u_j=p(u).
\]
The lifted $(n+1)$-minor sum is $1+\frac12\sum_i|b_i|=s(u)$.
Restoring the factors $(nV)^n$ and $(nV)^{n+1}$ in the two sums and
using~\eqref{eq:invariant} proves~\eqref{eq:seedstate}.
\end{proof}

Our two-upper-facet seed is the following body with 49 vertices and 14 facets:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seed12}
 K_0=K\left(\frac{17}{100},\frac{24}{100},\frac{32}{100},
                    \frac{41}{100},\frac{53}{100},\frac{69}{100}\right).
\end{equation}
In integer form its upper inequalities are
\[
 \sum_i(100\pm B_i)x_i\le100,\qquad
 B=(-69,-53,-41,-32,-24,-17,17,24,32,41,53,69).
\]
Direct rational evaluation, independently checked from its geometry,
gives
\begin{align}
 I_0&=\frac{1031929453906486710787861599001}
               {2386952337826268810834539955652},\label{eq:exactI}\\
 p_0&=\frac{768}{25},\qquad s_0=\frac{84}{25},\qquad
 a_0=\frac7{64},\qquad R_0=g(12)I_0\frac{768}{25}.\label{eq:exactstate}
\end{align}
For comparison, write $K=K(1/10,2/10,3/10,4/10)$ for the simpler
eight-dimensional seed. It has 25 vertices and state
\[
 8!\vol_8(K)=\frac{231953}{378378},\qquad
 R_8(K)=14g(8)\frac{231953}{378378},\qquad a(K)=\frac17.
\]
Its initial ratio $R_8(K)/c_8=231953/243243$ is below one. Nevertheless,
the same recursive amplification already exceeds the asymptotic ceiling
for $\CC$. A seed's immediate projection-volume root therefore does not
by itself describe its value under repeated operation rules.

\section{A symmetric polygon-section seed}\label{sec:polygon}
We enlarge the seed family to sections with three more facets than their
dimension. Let $A(x,y)=(-y,x+y)$ and let $b_1,\ldots,b_{18}$ be
the columns
\[
 A^j(10,0),\quad A^j(7,4),\quad A^j(4,7),\qquad 0\le j<6.
\]
Write $B=(b_1,\ldots,b_{18})$ and define the rational polytope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polygon-seed}
 S_0=\left\{x\in\R^{18}:x_i\ge0,\quad
                    \sum_i x_i=1,\quad Bx=0\right\}.
\end{equation}
Its affine hull has dimension fifteen. Choosing fifteen free coordinates
gives a rational affine realization in $\R^{15}$. Every coordinate
inequality defines a facet: deleting any column leaves two independent
antipodal pairs, so zero remains in the interior of the convex hull of
the remaining columns. The family of simplex sections and the use of
complementary minors follow the approach of~\cite[Section 4]{feng};
polygon configurations themselves are not claimed as new.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:polygon}
The seed $S_0$ has eighteen facets and exact state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polygon-state}
 a(S_0)=\frac19,\qquad
 R_{15}(S_0)=g(15)\frac{4877231181816111466}{252095850530248725}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $M=\binom{\boldsymbol1^T}{B}$, $F=\ker M$, $\lambda=\boldsymbol1/18$,
and $V=\vol_{15}(S_0)$. For an orthonormal frame $U$ of $F$, write the
facet area-normal vectors as $-d_iP_Fe_i$. Their balance implies
$d\in F^\perp=\operatorname{span}(\boldsymbol1,B^T)$.
The permutation $\sigma$ with $b_{\sigma(i)}=-b_i$ preserves the
section and gives $d_i=d_{\sigma(i)}$. Since $B$ has rank two, this forces
$d=t\boldsymbol1$. Taking the origin at $\lambda$, the divergence
identity gives $t=15V$. This permutation symmetry does not imply central
symmetry of $S_0$.

Define the two finite determinant sums
\[
 D=\sum_{i<j<k}|\det(b_j-b_i,b_k-b_i)|=101088,\qquad
 E=\sum_{i<j}|\det(b_i,b_j)|=11232.
\]
Complementary minors of $U$ and $[U,\lambda]$, using $B\boldsymbol1=0$,
give respectively $D/\sqrt{\det(MM^T)}$ and
$E/\sqrt{\det(MM^T)}$ for their maximal-minor sums.
The facet determinant identities therefore yield
\[
 R_{15}(S_0)=\frac{15^{15}VD}{\sqrt{\det(MM^T)}},\qquad a(S_0)=E/D.
\]
Let $f$ be the two-dimensional density of
$Z=\sum_i b_iX_i$, where the $X_i$ are independent mean-one exponential
variables. Coarea and scaling of the fibers $Mx=(s,0)$ give
\[
 f(0)=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}
                \frac{s^{15}V}{\sqrt{\det(MM^T)}}\,ds
      =\frac{15!V}{\sqrt{\det(MM^T)}}.
\]
This is the standard multivariate-spline volume method; see~\cite{xu}.
Thus $R_{15}(S_0)=g(15)Df(0)$.

For completeness, the supplied certificate evaluates the density by a
finite rational identity. For every independent pair $Y=(b_i,b_j)$, put
$\theta_Y=Y^{-T}\boldsymbol1$ and
$\alpha_Y=\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}}(1-\theta_Y\cdot b_k)^{-1}$.
No three specified columns are affinely collinear. The checker verifies
all coefficients of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polygon-laplace}
 1=\sum_Y\alpha_Y\prod_{k\notin Y}(1+w\cdot b_k).
\end{equation}
Dividing by the common product identifies the Laplace transform of $Z$
with a finite signed sum of transforms of $Y(X_i,X_j)$.
Uniqueness of these transforms gives their density identity.
The original density is continuous: two independent antipodal pairs
already give a bounded continuous planar density, and convolution with
the remaining pairs preserves continuity. Taking a generic ray $h$ to
zero consequently gives
\[
 f(0)=\sum_{Y^{-1}h>0}\frac{\alpha_Y}{|\det Y|}
     =\frac{2438615590908055733}{12741932669200891556400}.
\]
The certificate checks three distinct generic rays, with the same exact
answer. Substitution proves~\eqref{eq:polygon-state}.
\end{proof}


\section{A general section seed with stronger growth}\label{sec:general-section}
The symmetry above permits particularly short coordinates, but retaining
the facet weights allows a larger improvement. Let $C$ be the integer
$4\times23$ matrix specified in Appendix~\ref{app:integer-seed}, and put
\[
 G_0=\{x\in\R^{23}:x_i\ge0,\quad \boldsymbol1\cdot x=1,\quad Cx=0\}.
\]
It has dimension eighteen and twenty-three genuine facets. These claims
are checked by exact minors: every four columns are linearly independent,
every five are affinely independent, and deleting any column leaves a
simplex of columns containing zero strictly in its interior.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:general-state}
The seed $G_0$ satisfies the rational enclosures
\begin{align}
 \frac{108996733071}{10^{12}}\le a(G_0)
    &\le\frac{108996733072}{10^{12}},\label{eq:general-a}\\
 \frac{194095847511843177}{10^9}\le R_{18}(G_0)
    &\le\frac{194095847511843178}{10^9}.\label{eq:general-R}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We give the geometric identities needed to interpret the exact interval
certificate. For a rank-$r$ matrix $B$ with $m=n+r+1$ columns, let
$S(B)=\{x\ge0:\boldsymbol1\cdot x=1,Bx=0\}$, $M=\binom{\boldsymbol1^T}{B}$,
and let $f$ be the density of $\sum_i b_iX_i$ for independent mean-one
exponentials. Suppose zero lies in the interior of the convex hull
after each column deletion, and the density is $C^1$ at zero. Set
\begin{align*}
 \gamma_i&=1+\frac{b_i\cdot\nabla f(0)}{f(0)},\\
 D_\gamma&=\sum_{|J|=r+1}|\det M_J|\prod_{i\notin J}\gamma_i,\qquad
 E_\gamma=\sum_{|J|=r}|\det B_J|\prod_{i\notin J}\gamma_i.
\end{align*}
Here $M_J$ and $B_J$ retain the indicated columns. The weighted form of
the section calculation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-section}
 R_n(S(B))=g(n)f(0)D_\gamma,\qquad a(S(B))=E_\gamma/D_\gamma.
\end{equation}
Indeed, let $V=\vol_n(S(B))$, $F=\ker M$, and write its facet vectors
as $-d_iP_Fe_i$. Coarea gives $f(0)=n!V/\sqrt{\det(MM^T)}$.
If $f_i$ omits the $i$th exponential summand, integration by parts gives
$f_i(0)=f(0)+b_i\cdot\nabla f(0)$. The facet coarea identity and
\[
 \det(M_{-i}M_{-i}^T)=\det(MM^T)\|P_Fe_i\|^2
\]
therefore give $d_i=nV\gamma_i$. Applying complementary minors to an
orthonormal frame of $F$ and its lift by any $\lambda$ with
$M\lambda=(1,0)$ proves~\eqref{eq:weighted-section}.
These are the classical section and spline volume tools~\cite{feng,xu},
with the facet weights retained.

For the specified $C$, the required $C^1$ regularity follows directly
from Fourier inversion. Any unit vector is orthogonal to at most three
columns, so compactness gives a uniform positive lower bound for the
fourth smallest absolute scalar product. The characteristic function
$\prod_i(1+\mathrm i\,b_i\cdot t)^{-1}$ consequently decays as
$O((1+\|t\|)^{-20})$. It and its first frequency moments are integrable
in four dimensions. The same argument applies after deleting a column.

The density and gradient use the same finite Laplace identity as above,
now for four-column bases $Y$. All $8855$ linear and $33649$ affine
bases are nonsingular. To justify the identity without a large
coefficient list, put $\theta_Y=Y^{-T}\boldsymbol1$ and
$\alpha_Y=\prod_{k\notin Y}(1-\theta_Y\cdot b_k)^{-1}$.
The polynomials $\alpha_Y\prod_{k\notin Y}(1+w\cdot b_k)$ take the
values $\delta_{YZ}$ at the nodes $w=-\theta_Z$. Their number is
$\binom mr$, the dimension of polynomials of degree at most $m-r$
in $r$ variables. They form an interpolation basis and sum to one.
Laplace uniqueness and $C^1$ regularity thus give, for a generic ray $h$,
\[
 f(0)=\sum_{Y^{-1}h>0}\frac{\alpha_Y}{|\det Y|},\qquad
 \nabla f(0)=-\sum_{Y^{-1}h>0}\frac{\alpha_Y\theta_Y}{|\det Y|}.
\]
The checker encloses each rational summand outwards at denominator
$2^{256}$, then encloses the positive facet weights and their products
at denominator $2^{128}$. It repeats on two generic rays, checks
overlap in every component, and substitutes the resulting intervals
in~\eqref{eq:weighted-section}. Coarsening only outwards gives
\eqref{eq:general-a}--\eqref{eq:general-R}.
No floating-point value is an acceptance premise.
\end{proof}

\section{Recursive amplification and exact tail control}
Starting from a seed $X_0$ chosen from the four specified asymptotic seeds,
apply the prior scheme
of~\cite[Section 9]{mxym-calculus}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursion}
 X_{j+1}=(X_j\times X_j)*(X_j\times X_j),\qquad
 d_j=\dim X_j,\quad R_j=R_{d_j}(X_j),\quad a_j=a(X_j).
\end{equation}
The inherited identities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-recurrence}
 d_j=\frac{(3d_0+1)4^j-1}{3},\qquad a_j=\frac{a_0}{2^j},\qquad
 R_{j+1}=R_j^4D_j,\quad
 D_j=2a_j\frac{g(4d_j+1)}{g(2d_j)^2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact seed ranking at fixed dimension]\label{prop:ranking}
For any two full-dimensional polytope seeds $A,B$ of the same positive dimension $d$,
the recursion~\eqref{eq:recursion} has positive root limits
$\beta_A,\beta_B$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ranking}
 \left(\frac{\beta_A}{\beta_B}\right)^{3d+1}
 =\frac{a(A)R_d(A)^3}{a(B)R_d(B)^3}.
\end{equation}
Thus maximizing the limiting rate in the fixed-dimensional family
$K(u)$ is exactly equivalent to maximizing the rational objective
\[
 J(u)=I(u)^3p(u)^2s(u).
\]
This ranking concerns the specified recursion, not every operation schedule.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The central-binomial bounds used below imply that $D_j$ lies between
two positive constants depending only on $d,a_0$. The logarithmic
recurrence therefore gives a finite root limit for every seed.
For equal-dimensional seeds their multiplier ratio is $a(A)/a(B)$
at every level, so exact iteration gives
\[
 \frac{R_j(A)}{R_j(B)}
 =\left(\frac{R_d(A)}{R_d(B)}\right)^{4^j}
  \left(\frac{a(A)}{a(B)}\right)^{(4^j-1)/3}.
\]
Take $d_j$th roots and use $4^j/d_j\to3/(3d+1)$ to prove~\eqref{eq:ranking}.
Finally $aR^3=g(d)^3I^3p^2s$ by~\eqref{eq:seedstate}.
The factor $g(d)^3$ is constant at fixed dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tail}
For the three preceding seeds put $c=7$, and for $G_0$ put $c=8$.
For every $j\ge0$, one has $3<D_j<c$ and
$R_j^{1/d_j}<3$. The roots $R_j^{1/d_j}$ strictly increase to a limit
$\beta$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-enclosure}
 (3R_j^3)^{1/(3d_j+1)}<\beta<(cR_j^3)^{1/(3d_j+1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We extend the elementary tail argument in~\cite[Lemma 9.1]{mxym-calculus}
to these seed states. For $q\ge1$,
\[
 \frac{4^q}{2\sqrt q}\le\binom{2q}{q}
 \le\frac{4^q}{\sqrt{3q+1}}.
\]
Both follow by induction from equality at $q=1$.
For the lower bound the squared induction comparison has difference
$(2q+1)^2-4q(q+1)=1$; for the upper bound it has difference
$4(q+1)^2(3q+1)-(2q+1)^2(3q+4)=q$.
Set $q=2d_j$ and note that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binomial}
 \frac{g(2q+1)}{g(q)^2}
 =\frac{4^q}{\binom{2q}{q}}
     \left(1+\frac1{2q}\right)^{2q}.
\end{equation}
The last factor is strictly between $2$ and $e$. Thus
\[
 4a_j\sqrt{6d_j+1}<D_j<4ea_j\sqrt{2d_j}.
\]
The closed dimension formula implies
\[
 a_j^2(6d_j+1)=a_0^2(6d_0+2-4^{-j})\ge a_0^2(6d_0+1),
\]
and $a_j^2(2d_j)<a_0^2(2d_0+2/3)$.
The exact checks
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hypotheses}
 16a_0^2(6d_0+1)>9,\qquad
 121a_0^2(2d_0+2/3)<c^2,\qquad
 cR_0^3<3^{3d_0+1}
\end{equation}
hold for all four seeds; for $G_0$, use the lower endpoint of
\eqref{eq:general-a} in the first comparison and the upper state endpoints in the other two. The elementary bound $e<11/4$ proves $D_j<c$.
Iterating $R_{j+1}<cR_j^4$ gives
\[
 R_j^3\le\frac{(cR_0^3)^{4^j}}c
 <\frac{3^{(3d_0+1)4^j}}c
 =\frac{3^{3d_j+1}}c<3^{3d_j}.
\]
Since $D_j>3>R_j^{1/d_j}$, the recurrence proves strict root increase.
The upper bound by three proves convergence. Iteration of the logarithmic
recurrence and the dimension formula yield the absolutely convergent series
\[
 \log\beta=
 \frac{\log R_j+\sum_{t\ge0}4^{-t-1}\log D_{j+t}}{d_j+1/3}.
\]
Using $3<D_{j+t}<c$ and $\sum_{t\ge0}4^{-t-1}=1/3$ proves
the two strict bounds in~\eqref{eq:tail-enclosure}.
\end{proof}

\section{The imported upper bound and separation from dimension nine}
The mixed Bellman theorem~\cite{mxym-upper} states, for every $K\in\CC$,
with $D=n+1$ and $H=1/a(K)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prior-upper}
 \log Q(K)\le\alpha\left(D-\frac{H^2}{D}\right)
                 +\delta\left(D-\frac{H^4}{D^3}\right),
 \qquad\alpha=\frac{87}{2000},\quad\delta=\frac{11}{2000}.
\end{equation}
This is an imported theorem, not a new inequality of this note.
Its pinned exact certificate was replayed successfully: 19,900 finite
state rectangles, 15,568 tail cells, and the analytic large-dimension
constants. The complete English argument was also inspected. Exact
replay checks its finite proof obligations; it does not replace review
of its analytic argument.

Discarding the dependence on $H$ gives the coarse bound
$R_n(K)<1.051(n+1)(2.8548)^n$. Combined with a single block and simplex
padding, this gave the earlier sufficient threshold $15500$.
Retaining that dependence yields a much smaller comparison bound.
Write
\[
 f_n(H)=\log H+\alpha(D-H^2/D)+\delta(D-H^4/D^3).
\]
Then $R_n(K)\le g(n)\exp f_n(H)$. For every rational $h>0$,
$f_n''(H)\le-2\alpha/D$ implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent-upper}
 \sup_{K\in\CC_n}R_n(K)
 \le g(n)h\exp\left[
 \alpha(D-h^2/D)+\delta(D-h^4/D^3)
       +\frac{D}{4\alpha}
        \left(\frac1h-\frac{2\alpha h}{D}
                         -\frac{4\delta h^3}{D^3}\right)^2\right].
\end{equation}
Indeed, the quadratic upper Taylor bound at $h$ is maximized by
completing the square. Rational centres near its stationary point give
tight bounds without solving an algebraic equation in proof acceptance.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:uniform-upper}
For every $n\ge49$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-upper}
 \sup_{K\in\CC_n}R_n(K)
 <\frac9{10}\left(\frac{7137}{2500}\right)^n.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Drop the nonpositive quartic term in $f_n(H)$ and use
$x e^{-x}\le e^{-1}$, with $x=2\alpha H^2/D$. This gives
\[
 R_n(K)^2\le\frac{1000}{87}(n+1)g(n)^2
                  \exp\left(\frac{49}{500}(n+1)-1\right).
\]
The sequence $B_n=(n+1)g(n)^2e^{-2n}$ strictly decreases. Its consecutive
ratio is $(n+2)(1+1/n)^{2n}/((n+1)e^2)$, which is below one since
\[
 2n\log(1+1/n)<2-\frac1{n+1},\qquad
 \log(1+1/(n+1))<\frac1{n+1}.
\]
The first inequality follows by integrating
$t/(1+t)\ge t/(1+x)$ on $0\le t\le x$, with $x=1/n$.
Consequently, for $n\ge49$,
\[
 R_n(K)^2\le\frac{1000}{87}B_{49}
                  e^{-451/500}e^{(1049/500)n}.
\]
Rational Taylor bounds certify
\[
 \frac{1000}{87}\,50g(49)^2e^{-98-451/500}<\frac{81}{100},
 \qquad e^{1049/1000}<\frac{7137}{2500}.
\]
Taking positive square roots proves the claim. In particular
$\Gamma_{\CC}<2.855$, as used in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

Two additional rational members of the inherited seed family suffice
for the finite witnesses. The ten-dimensional seed is
\[
 S_{10}=K(16/1000,51/1000,91/1000,142/1000,217/1000).
\]
The nine-dimensional seed $S_9$ is a pyramid over
$K(1/100,2/100,3/100,5/100)$ in dimension eight; equivalently, append one
zero coefficient to its paired tilt vector. It has 26 vertices and 11
facets, whereas $S_{10}$ has 36 vertices and 12 facets. The calculus in
$(H,Q)$ extends to a point with the formal values $H=Q=1$:
\begin{align}
 (H,Q)(A*B)&=(H_A+H_B,Q_AQ_B),\label{eq:HQjoin}\\
 H(A\times B)&=\frac{r+s}{r/H_A+s/H_B},\qquad
 Q(A\times B)=Q_AQ_B\frac{g(r)g(s)}{g(r+s)}
                            \frac{sH_A+rH_B}{r+s}.\label{eq:HQproduct}
\end{align}
The point case in~\eqref{eq:HQjoin} is the ordinary pyramid formula;
products with a point can be omitted. These are rewritings of the prior
operation rules, not new geometric identities.

Set $W_9=S_9$. The geometric family and the dimension-nine simplex

\section{Discussion}
The separation in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} identifies a limitation of the
point-generated class rather than a failure of products and joins
themselves. The same inherited operations amplify the new seeds beyond
the upper rate available to every finite expression in $\CC$.
Thus changing the initial geometric input can matter even when the
subsequent construction rules remain fixed. The result compares complete
construction classes; exceeding one previously known recursive sequence
alone would not establish this conclusion.

The eight-dimensional example illustrates why immediate seed performance
can be misleading. Its ratio $R_8/c_8$ is below one, yet its recursively
amplified rate exceeds the ceiling for $\CC$. The lifted invariant $a$
records information used by joins that the initial value of $R$ alone
does not capture. Proposition~\ref{prop:ranking} makes this observation
quantitative: at a fixed dimension and for the specified recursion, the
correct seed objective is $aR^3$, or equivalently $I^3p^2s$ in the stated
tilt family. This supplies an exact target for further seed search.
It does not justify comparing different dimensions by that objective or
claiming that the balanced recursion is the best possible schedule.

The general section seed raises the certified amplified lower rate to
$2.928$, compared with $2.890$ for the polygon section and $2.861$ for
the two-upper-facet seed, using the same recursion. Its twenty-three
facets are five more than its dimension. Retaining the nonuniform facet
weights allows a larger improvement, at the cost of less compact seed
coordinates. The polygon example offers a simpler symmetric comparison. Its eighteen facets are only three more than its dimension, but their planar section
constraints allow geometric inputs outside the preceding two-upper-facet
family. This shows a concrete benefit from broadening the seed search.
These certificates establish particular witnesses and their rates.
Optimality of either family and necessity of the additional facets remain open.

The finite and asymptotic objectives favour different seeds. The comparison
in every dimension at least nine uses the invariant dependence in the
class envelope and finitely many checked product--join witnesses. The
eighteen-dimensional section instead supplies the stronger asymptotic rate.
The threshold nine is not proved minimal: the present result makes no new
assertion about unrestricted maximizers in dimensions four through eight.
Nor does the lower rate approach a known unrestricted optimum: the
classical upper root $\pi e/2$ leaves a substantial gap.

Explicitness also needs a computational qualification. The block $L$
has a compact description by 224 facet inequalities but more than
46 million vertices. Its certified ratio is obtained from the checked
seed geometry and the inherited operation identities, rather than by
enumerating its vertices or constructing its projection body directly.
The package therefore makes these particular witnesses and their
comparison independently checkable; it does not provide an efficient
algorithm for optimizing projection-body volume over arbitrary polytopes.

The whole-class upper theorem~\cite{mxym-upper} is an essential external
dependency. Its exact finite obligations were replayed, but this does not
formalize its analytic argument or constitute external peer review.
Relative to the inspected sources, the contribution is the specified
rational seeds, their certified amplified rates, and the resulting
class separation and effective comparison. The underlying section,
spline, and operation methods remain attributed to prior work; the
literature audit does not establish bibliographic priority.

Zhang's subsequent positive sextic Bellman envelope~\cite{mxym-sextic}
reports the sharper class ceiling $\Gamma_{\CC}<2.85386$. This further
upper-bound refinement is compatible with the separation proved here.
Our proof and finite comparison certificates retain the earlier pinned
quadratic/quartic theorem~\cite{mxym-upper}, whose finite obligations are
replayed in the release; the newer sextic result is cited as related work.

A structural bound for $aR^3$ at fixed dimension would distinguish a
good seed from an optimum. Broader section families, different seed
dimensions, and unbalanced operation schedules are concrete directions
for improving the lower rate. Sharper class envelopes could reduce the
finite certificate or improve separation margins. Moving below dimension
nine would require a new unrestricted counterexample. These problems
remain unresolved by the present certificates.

\section{Conclusion}
An explicit rational eighteen-dimensional simplex-section seed, amplified by the prior
product--join recursion, gives a projection-volume root limit strictly
between $2.9280$ and $2.9283$. Combined with the attributed upper theorem
for the point-generated class, this proves a strict exponential
separation from that entire class. A finite rational operation
certificate and a repeating 197-dimensional block give a comparison in
every dimension $n\ge9$. One explicit sequence satisfies this finite
separation while retaining the stronger root limit.

The examples show that the initial seed and its lifted invariant are
material to recursive growth. Exact geometry, recurrence certificates,
and a finite comparison make the claims reproducible without treating
numerical search as proof. The unrestricted extremal rate, optimal seed
and schedule, and minimal separation dimension remain open.

\section{A simplex truncation identity}\label{app:truncation}
The following is a standard simplex section formula; see
Lasserre~\cite[Theorem 2.2]{lasserre} and the Genocchi--Hermite
representation of divided differences~\cite[Section 9]{deboor}.
We use the following normalization.
Let $t_0,\ldots,t_n$ be distinct real numbers, and let
$\Delta_n=\{y_i\ge0:\sum_{i=1}^n y_i\le1\}$, with
$y_0=1-\sum_{i=1}^n y_i$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncation}
 \frac{\vol_n\{y\in\Delta_n:\sum_{i=0}^n t_i y_i\ge0\}}
      {\vol_n(\Delta_n)}
 =\sum_{t_i>0}\frac{t_i^n}{\prod_{j\ne i}(t_i-t_j)}.
\end{equation}
Here a zero node contributes zero.
