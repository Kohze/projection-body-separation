1. Geometry and smoothness

matrix B are in seed.json. Put M=[1;B] and
  S={x>=0: sum x_i=1, Bx=0}.
The checker verifies every r-column linear determinant and every
(r+1)-column affine determinant is nonzero. It finds strictly positive
barycentric solutions for 3113 simplices on the columns. For each deleted
column i, at least one of these simplices omits i. Thus zero lies in the
interior of the convex hull of every remaining column set. S has dimension
n in its affine hull, and every coordinate inequality is a genuine facet.
Selecting n free coordinates gives a rational affine realization in R^n.

Let f be the density of Z=sum b_i X_i, with independent mean-one
exponentials X_i. It is continuously differentiable. To see this directly,
any unit direction is orthogonal to at most r-1 columns, since every r
columns are independent. By compactness the r-th smallest |b_i dot u|
has a positive uniform lower bound. The characteristic function
  product_i (1+i b_i dot t)^-1
therefore decays uniformly as O((1+|t|)^-(m-r+1)). Since m-r+1=20>r+1,
it and its first frequency moments are integrable. Fourier inversion
gives a C^1 density. The same argument applies after deleting any column.

2. Weighted geometric state

Write V=vol_n(S), F=ker M, and p_i=P_F e_i. For the facet x_i=0,
let its area-normal vector be -d_i p_i. Coarea and homogeneity give
  f(0)=n! V/sqrt(det MM^T).
If f_i is the density with column i deleted, the corresponding facet
formula and the Gram determinant identity are
  f_i(0)=(n-1)! area(facet i)/sqrt(det M_-i M_-i^T),
  det(M_-i M_-i^T)=det(MM^T) |p_i|^2.
Integration by parts on the nonnegative coordinate x_i, equivalently
as a distributional identity followed by C^1 regularity, yields
  f_i(z)=f(z)+b_i dot grad f(z).
Consequently
  gamma_i=1+b_i dot grad f(0)/f(0), d_i=n V gamma_i.

Choose an orthonormal frame U of F and a positive lambda with M lambda=(1,0).
In these affine coordinates the lifted facet vectors are
d_i(-U_i,lambda_i). Complementary maximal minors give the weighted sums
  D_gamma=sum_{|J|=r+1} |det M_J| product_{i notin J} gamma_i,
  E_gamma=sum_{|J|=r} |det B_J| product_{i notin J} gamma_i.
The minor scale in both sums is 1/sqrt(det MM^T). For the lifted minors,
use B lambda=0 and 1^T lambda=1; lambda's component in F does not affect
the determinant. The classical facet determinant formulas imply
  R_n(S)=g(n) f(0) D_gamma, a(S)=E_gamma/D_gamma,
where g(n)=n^n/n!. These are the section/coarea/spline methods, with the
facet weights retained; no new general method or extremal theorem is claimed.

3. Density and gradient by finite multivariate interpolation

For each r-column basis Y, let theta_Y=Y^-T 1 and
  alpha_Y=product_{k notin Y}(1-theta_Y dot b_k)^-1.
General position, checked by the determinants above, guarantees that
all denominators are nonzero. The polynomials
  L_Y(w)=alpha_Y product_{k notin Y}(1+w dot b_k)
have degree m-r. At each arrangement node w=-theta_Z their values are
delta_{Y,Z}. There are binom(m,r) polynomials, exactly the dimension of
the polynomial space of total degree at most m-r in r variables. They
therefore form an interpolation basis and sum identically to 1.
This is a classical interpolation argument, not a new identity method.

Dividing this polynomial identity by the common product identifies the
Laplace transform of Z with a finite signed sum of transforms of Y X_Y.
All transforms exist near zero. Uniqueness for finite signed measures
therefore gives the corresponding density identity. On a generic ray h,
C^1 regularity justifies the limits
  f(0)=sum_{Y^-1 h>0} alpha_Y/|det Y|,
  grad f(0)=-sum_{Y^-1 h>0} alpha_Y theta_Y/|det Y|.
The checker verifies that each supplied ray is generic and reconstructs
both sums on two rays. Their outward intervals overlap in every component.
