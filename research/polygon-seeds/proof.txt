Analytic proof for the rational 15-dimensional seed
10 October 2026; no minimal-dimension or bibliographic-priority claim.

Let A(x,y)=(-y,x+y), and let B be the 18 columns
  A^j(10,0), A^j(7,4), A^j(4,7), 0<=j<6.
Let M be the 3 by 18 matrix with first row all ones and remaining rows B.
F=ker M has dimension n=15. The rational polytope is
  S={x>=0: sum_i x_i=1, Bx=0},
in the affine Euclidean space 1/18+F. It has 18 facets.

All columns occur with their negatives, span R^2, and sum to zero.
Deleting any column leaves two independent antipodal pairs of the six
axis-orbit columns. Hence zero remains in the interior of their convex
hull. This proves that S is full-dimensional in its affine space and
that every coordinate inequality defines a facet.

Let U be an orthonormal 18 by 15 frame for F, V=vol_15(S), and p_i=P_F e_i.
Write the area-normal vector of facet i as -d_i p_i. The balance identity
implies d in F-perp=span{1, rows B}, so d=t1+B^T v. The permutation swapping
each column with its negative preserves S and its metric, and sends p_i
to p_-i. Thus d_i=d_-i, forcing B^T v=0 and d=t1. With origin 1/18,
the facet support product is d_i/18. The divergence identity gives t=nV.
This is a permutation symmetry, not central symmetry of S.

Define
 D=sum_{i<j<k} |det(b_j-b_i,b_k-b_i)|,
 E=sum_{i<j} |det(b_i,b_j)|.
Complementary maximal minors give
 sum_{|I|=n}|det U_I|=D/sqrt(det MM^T),
 sum_{|I|=n+1}|det[U,1/18]_I|=E/sqrt(det MM^T).
For the second identity use B1=0 and an orthonormal row frame for B;
the missing scale is ||1/18||=1/sqrt(18).
The facet determinant formulas therefore imply
 R_n(S)=n^n V D/sqrt(det MM^T), a(S)=E/D.

Let Z=sum_i b_i E_i for independent mean-one exponential E_i, with
two-dimensional density f. Coarea for Mx=(s,0), followed by homogeneity
of the fiber volume, gives
 f(0)=n! V/sqrt(det MM^T).
Consequently R_n(S)=g(n)D f(0) and a(S)=E/D.

The density is continuous at the origin. For example, the independent
antipodal column pairs generate independent two-sided exponential
variables along their directions. Two independent pairs already have
a continuous bounded two-dimensional density; convolution with the
remaining independent variables preserves continuity.

Exact evaluation of f does not rely on optimization. For each independent
pair Y=(b_i,b_j), set theta_Y=Y^-T 1 and
 alpha_Y=product_{k notin Y}(1-theta_Y dot b_k)^-1.
No three columns of this specified B are affinely collinear (checked).
The finite identity
  1= sum_Y alpha_Y product_{k notin Y}(1+w dot b_k)
is checked coefficient by coefficient over Q, including pairs of zero
determinant being omitted. Dividing by the common product proves an
identity of Laplace transforms of finite signed measures. Each term is
the transform of alpha_Y times the push-forward of two independent
exponentials under Y. All transforms exist near w=0. Uniqueness therefore
gives their density identity. Along a generic ray h, its limit at zero is
  f(0)=sum_{Y^-1 h>0} alpha_Y/|det Y|.
Continuity of the original density justifies this limit. Repeating with
different generic rays is an independent acceptance cross-check.
